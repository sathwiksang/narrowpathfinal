by Dr. Sunny Ezhumattoor (John Mathew)

For two millennia, most teachers have placed the birth of the New Testament Church in the Upper Room (Acts 2). Dr. Sunny Ezhumattoor offers a careful, Scripture-first re-examination that addresses the questions readers keep asking but rarely see answered: Where could thousands hear Peter at once? How were 3,000 baptized in a single day? Without forcing new doctrine or division, this concise study brings clarity by pointing to the Jerusalem Temple as the most plausible setting of Pentecost.

What sets this work apart?

Text that flows, not fragments

Reading Acts 1–2 as a continuous narrative keeps the focus on the apostles ("Peter with the eleven"), and fits naturally with Luke's note that the disciples were continually in the Temple after the Ascension.

"House" as God's House

The "house" that filled with the sound and the Spirit can naturally refer to the House of God (Temple), a common biblical usage that resolves the "Upper Room crowd size" problem.

Real capacity, real baptisms

The Temple complex—especially Solomon's Porch—could host enormous festival crowds, and the area around the Mount was ringed with mikva'ot (ritual immersion pools), making the baptism of 3,000 logistically credible.

Respectful, pastoral tone

Dr. Sunny's aim is not argument but illumination—offering readers satisfying, evidence-based answers on a question that is not a salvation issue, yet matters for understanding the Church's beginning.

Why read it now?

Because this booklet combines close exegesis, historical context, and archaeological insight in a way that's accessible and spiritually edifying. It invites believers to rejoice in what God did on Pentecost—in relationship with the Temple—and to anchor their confidence in Scripture.
